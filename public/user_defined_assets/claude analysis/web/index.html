    How the Pedago ERP works

    The sales lead module takes every inquiry Pedago Academy receives, gives it to one sales executive, makes sure it is called quickly and followed up on time, and confirms the enrollment only when the money is verified.

    One ownerA lead belongs to one executive at a time. Every move is deliberate and recorded.
    Fresh firstThe newest leads are served first, because a lead cools with every hour it waits.
    Small batchesAn executive holds a few leads, finishes them, and only then gets more.
    Promises keptA missed call-back is visible to managers and stops new leads.

    Figures such as “60 minutes” or “3 leads” are the default settings. An administrator can change them under Settings; the Documentation page inside the ERP always shows the current values.

    Lead life cycle

    A lead has exactly one status.

    1. NewIn the central queue, no owner
    2. AssignedWith an executive, not yet called
    3. Follow-up / InterestedCalled; a call-back or a decision is pending
    4. Pending paymentEnrollment created, money awaited
    5. Under reviewPayment submitted, Accounts checking
    6. ConvertedPayment verified

    Closed outcomes at any point after a call: Not Interested Unreachable Lost. A lead returned to the queue becomes New again and remembers its previous owner.

    Lead intake and duplicates

    • Leads arrive by CSV upload, single entry or quick-create, always under a campaign (channel, optional course, distribution mode).
    • Auto campaigns feed the engine. Manual campaigns wait for an administrator to assign their leads.
    • A lead whose phone number already exists, as any of its numbers, is not created twice. The inquiry is logged on the existing lead. A closed lead is reopened to its previous owner, an open lead gets a follow-up when it is a new day, and the entry also waits in Duplicate review in case it is a separate student such as a sibling.
    • A lead can hold extra numbers. Number check shows who owns a number before you add it. Paste leads takes spreadsheet rows, and an administrator can merge two records of one customer.
    • High-intent channels — phone, reference, WhatsApp, website, Google form — are served ahead of ad leads of the same temperature, because the person contacted Pedago first.

    Lead priority: hot, warm, cool, cold

    Studies of sales response times agree on one thing: the chance of reaching and qualifying a person is highest in the first minutes after they ask, drops sharply within the first hour, and keeps falling over the following days. The system is built around that.

    Warmup to 24 hours

    What the temperature decides

    • Distribution: hot leads go out first, then warm, newest first.
    • No lead is forgotten: one slot in every four of a cycle is kept for the longest-waiting cool or cold lead.
    • Calling order: on an executive’s task list, hot leads are at the top.
    • Supervision: team leaders see assigned leads that have had no first call, longest wait first.

    Distribution engine

    Leads are handed out in batches of up to n = 3. Let L be the leads waiting in Auto campaigns and S the executives available right now.

    • Available means: active sales executive, on shift, not in the last 30 minutes of the shift, no unfinished batch, not flagged Lazy.
    • Each available executive gets min(n, L ÷ S rounded down) leads.
    • If that is fewer than n, the leftover leads go one each to the executives who have been idle longest.
    • Leads are dealt one at a time in rounds, hottest first, so hot leads are spread fairly.

    Working a lead

    The executive’s My tasks page is one ranked list. Start at number 1.

    OrderWhatWhy
    1Hot new leadsMinutes matter most here
    2Call-backs that are due — interested customers first, then longest overdueA kept promise builds trust; a broken one loses the sale
    3Warm, then cool, then cold new leadsRemaining value falls with age
    4Call-backs due later todaySo the day can be planned

    After every call

    • Record the call result, the customer’s interest, a note, and the next action: Follow-up, Interested, Not Interested, Unreachable or Lost.
    • Follow-up requires a call-back date and time in the future.
    • Saving feedback sets the lead’s status, closes any earlier open follow-up, schedules the new one and frees the batch slot.
    • When every lead of the batch has feedback, the batch is complete and the executive is available for the next one.
    • Only the owner records calls. Managers can read the history.

    Follow-ups and the Lazy rule

    • Coming up: from 30 hours before its time until the exact time. The owner gets one reminder and it shows in blue on the dashboard.
    • Overdue: from the exact time until a new follow-up, a conversion or another outcome is logged. It shows in red, and the owner is told at the exact time and again every few hours.
    • An executive with a follow-up overdue for more than 24 hours is flagged Lazy and receives no new leads until it is handled.
    • An administrator can move an open follow-up, with its lead, to another executive, or return the lead to the central queue.

    Transfers

    1. The owner requests a transfer to an executive who is in a team, with a reason. The lead stays with the owner meanwhile.
    2. The leader of the sending team and the leader of the receiving team each approve. A team with no leader needs no approval from that side.
    3. If nobody decides within 7 days, the request is approved automatically.
    4. On approval the lead, its open follow-ups and its team move to the new owner, and the old owner’s batch slot is freed.

    An administrator can approve or reject any request at once. Rejection always needs a reason.

    Performance and KPIs

    Four KPIs show how well each sales person works the leads they are given. Targets are the same for everyone and are set in Settings.

    KPIHow it is measuredDefault target
    Calls per working dayLogged calls ÷ days in the period up to today, Fridays left out25
    First-call timeMedian wait from assignment to the first logged call (lower is better)30 min
    Follow-ups kept on timeCall-backs done within an hour of the promised time ÷ call-backs due90%
    Conversion rateLeads assigned in the period that have since converted ÷ leads assigned10%

    Green means the target is met, amber within a fifth of it, red further away. Sales people see their own KPIs and a six-week trend; leaders, supervisors and administrators see a table of their people.

    Leave

    • Everyone except developers can apply: type, first and last day, reason. No past start, no overlap with another request. Friday, the weekly holiday, is not counted.
    • Types follow the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006: casual 10 days a year, sick 14 (a doctor’s certificate above 3 days), annual 1 day per 18 worked after a year of service, maternity 16 weeks after 6 months, and unpaid.
    • Approval: a sales executive needs the team leader, then a supervisor or administrator. A team leader, accounts or marketing person needs one approval from a supervisor or administrator. A supervisor needs an administrator. An administrator needs none. A rejection ends the request and needs a reason.
    • While approved leave covers today the status is On Leave: no new leads, and the person shows on their manager’s dashboard.

    Enrollment, payments and installments

    • Converting a lead: the owner presses Enroll this student, picks the course, gives a discount within the limit and sets the installments. The lead waits as Enrolled - Pending Payment. The owner submits each payment with a transaction ID or slip.
    • Money is confirmed by Accounts (or an administrator), never by sales.
    • Verify: the installment becomes Paid, the student Enrolled, the lead Converted, and the executive’s batch slot is freed.
    • Reject: a reason is required; the installment, enrollment and lead return to awaiting payment.
    • Installments due within 2 days are listed as Due soon; unpaid installments past their date are Overdue.

    The customer payment page, online gateways and receipts are not built yet.

    Notifications

    • A bell at the top right and a Notifications page tell each role what it can act on. Opening one takes you to the work.
    • Sales: new leads, follow-ups coming up and overdue, transfer and payment results, conversions, leave decisions. Leaders and supervisors: transfers, Lazy people, leave to approve. Accounts: payments to verify. Marketing: campaign conversions. Administrators: duplicates, profile changes, payments, Lazy people, leave.
    • A reminder clears itself when the work is done.

    Notices are created by the scheduler, so it must be running.

    Assignment controls and tools

    • Each sales person is Auto or Manual. Manual people never receive engine leads. An optional daily limit caps what the engine gives in a day. Leads handed over by a person do not count.
    • A wrong-number call closes the lead as Invalid/Junk. A connected call that leaves the lead open needs a call-back time.
    • A lead can carry a priority (High, Medium, Low), a guardian name and a standing remark. High priority leads come first within their group.
    • Data health lists stuck leads and offers safe fixes. Thank on WhatsApp opens WhatsApp with a ready message.

    Passwords and confidentiality

    • By company policy, staff passwords are stored in a form an administrator can read back.
    • Reading or resetting a password requires the administrator’s own password and a written reason, and is recorded in a log that cannot be edited.
    • A reset gives a temporary password that must be changed at the next sign-in.
    • The secret key that protects the passwords lives only in the server configuration. Whoever holds both the database and the key can read every password, so both must be guarded.

    Open risk. The folder public/user_defined_assets holds real customer contacts and interim staff PINs, is reachable from the web, and is in the Git repository. Move it out of public/, change the PINs and remove the files from the repository history.

    Glossary

    Batch
    The small set of leads an executive holds at one time.
    Central queue
    New leads that nobody owns yet.
    Cycle
    One run of the distribution engine.
    Fresh / stale
    Fresh is hot or warm; stale is cool or cold.
    Follow-up
    A scheduled call-back to a lead.
    Lazy
    The flag on an executive whose follow-up is badly overdue.
    Slot
    One place in a batch. It is freed by feedback, a transfer, or a verified payment.
    Temperature
    Hot, warm, cool or cold — how long ago the lead was received.
